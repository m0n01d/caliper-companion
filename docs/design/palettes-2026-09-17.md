# Caliper Companion — palette proposals (2026-09-17)

Three colour palettes for the dark, phone-portrait bench UI, rendered on the chosen layout A
(`review-2026-09-17.md` §4, Gallery-first) so they can be compared before one is applied. One is
Fusion-inspired, two are original. Nothing here is applied: the app's `src/theme.css` is untouched.

**Renders** (all 390 wide @2x, in `docs/design/mockups/`): `compare-palettes.png` is the contact
sheet — current | Dark Sky | Night Bench | Concrete, each column the layout-A Part page above its
annotate overlay strip. Per palette: `palette-<name>.html/.png` (a copy of `part-a.html` with only
the `--cc-*` colour tokens and derived `--glass-*` overridden inline after the theme link) and
`overlay-<name>.html/.png` (the §5 / `Draw.res` overlay geometry in SVG over a light `#D9D4C7` and a
dark `#2A2C2F` photo). `overlay-current.*` is the same strip for today's tokens.

**Method.** Same 17 slots as DESIGN.md §2, plus `accent-pressed` (§6) and `error-ink`. Slot names
below are generic — `accent` = tappable, `live` = verified; the code's `amber`/`teal` token and
constant names would be renamed if a palette is adopted. Contrast is WCAG 2 relative luminance,
computed by a small node script (the same formula as `lum = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B` over
sRGB-linearised channels; ratio `(L1+0.05)/(L2+0.05)`). The overlay halo is that palette's `ground`
at 85 % (`Draw.res` `halo`, SPEC §8a A3), composited over the photo before measuring, and the
saved-dimension rows also apply §5's 60 % opacity to both halo and colour. Glass tokens follow
`liquid-glass-web.md` §3 verbatim: fill = surface @72 %, fill-strong = ground @82 %, stroke = text
@10 %, highlight = text @16 %.

## 1. Fusion / Autodesk colour research

Verified from primary sources:

- **Fusion product icon** (the current 2024 mark, `Autodesk_Fusion_Logo.svg` on Wikimedia Commons,
  read from the SVG source via `commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Autodesk_Fusion_Logo.svg`):
  fills `#FF6B00` (the badge), `#FF9548` (the lighter fold), `#933C00` (the fold shadow), `#FFFFFF`
  (the F). This is the orange the owner sees in his dock next to the app.
- **Autodesk brand hub, current identity** (https://brand.autodesk.com/visual-system/color/): Black
  `#000000`, White `#FFFFFF`, Hello Yellow `#FFFF00`; greys Warm Slate `#D5D5CB`, Slate `#666666`;
  tertiary Dawn `#F09D4F`, Dusk `#F2520A`, Twilight `#1D91D0`, Morning `#2AD0A9`. Note: since the
  2021 rebrand there is no "Autodesk blue" in the primary set — the blue survives only as Twilight.
- **Autodesk product-design palette, older** (https://brand-stg.autodesk.com/brand-elements/colors):
  autodeskBlue-500 `#0696D7` with tints 100 `#CDEAF7`, 300 `#6AC0E7`, 400 `#38ABDF`, 900 `#0A324D`;
  charcoal 900 `#3C3C3C`, 800 `#4D4D4D`, 700 `#666666`; turquoise-500 `#32BCAD`; yellowOrange-500
  `#FAA21B`; red-500 `#EB5555`, 400 `#F48686`; darkBlue-600 `#1858A8`. This is the "Autodesk blue"
  people mean.
- **Sketch colours** (Autodesk support, "What do the line colors mean in Fusion sketch", via search
  summary — the article body itself returned 403 to the sandbox): blue = unconstrained geometry,
  black (white in dark environments) = fully constrained, orange dashed = construction, purple =
  projected, green = fixed, yellow = failed projection.
- **Themes and environments** (Autodesk support "How to change theme in Autodesk Fusion" via the
  help.autodesk.com mirror; third-party guides at cadforum.cz and autocadeverything.com): Preferences
  › General › Theme = *Light gray* / *Dark Blue* / *Fusion classic*; viewport environments include
  *Dark Sky* (matches the dark UI), *Gray Room* (neutral dark grey), *Tranquility Blue* ("very dark
  blue, almost black"). Backgrounds are RGB values in environment XML files; no hexes are published.

**Not verified** (treat as approximations): the actual hex of the *Dark Blue* theme's panels and
toolbar, the selection / pre-selection highlight blue, and the timeline colours. Every autodesk.com
support page returned 403 from the sandbox, and the only community numbers found
(github.com/OmegaRogue/FusionStyle: `--main-bg-color #323232`, fg `#C0C0C6`, border `#2C2C2C`,
active `#354F85`, link `#287BDE`) are a user theme for the Fusion Team *web dashboard*, not the
desktop app. Palette 1's greys are therefore derived from the environment names, the charcoal
tints and the "almost black, dark blue" description — not sampled from Fusion.

## 2. Palette 1 — Dark Sky (Fusion-inspired), cool · accent `#FF7F2A` · live `#5AC1F2`

**Concept.** Working inside Fusion's dark viewport: a near-black with a blue cast (Tranquility Blue /
Dark Sky), panels at the current tonal steps but cool, the Fusion icon orange for everything you
tap, the sketch/Autodesk blue for everything live or verified.

**Derivation.** `ground #15181D` is the current `#17181A` pushed two points blue; surface, surface-2,
field and border keep the current luminance steps. `accent #FF7F2A` is the icon's `#FF6B00` lifted
8 L so it clears 7:1 on ground (the icon orange itself becomes `accent-pressed` — the button "goes
Fusion" under the thumb). `live #5AC1F2` sits between autodeskBlue-300 and -400 and is the closest
dark-UI-safe reading of the unconstrained-sketch blue; `live-ink` is autodeskBlue-100 exactly, wash
and border are the -900 family. `error #F47070` is the red-400/500 family, pinker than the orange so
the accent/error hue gap (~24°) matches today's amber/error gap.

**App icon.** Orange jaws + blue bar on a dark-sky square still composes, but it now reads as an
Autodesk-family icon; regenerate (`scripts/make-icons.mjs` hardcodes `GROUND/AMBER/TEAL`) and use
`#FF7F2A`, not `#FF6B00`, on the icon so it is kin to Fusion, not a copy.

**Honest downside.** It borrows a brand: the app stops having its own colour identity, and orange is
the lowest accent-vs-halo of the four on a light photo (4.95:1 vs 5.97 today — still fine over a
5 px halo, but the least margin). Orange is also the hardest of the three accents to keep apart
from error red.

## 3. Palette 2 — Night Bench (original), warm · accent `#E8B84D` · live `#66D68A`

**Concept.** A bench lamp at night: warm near-black, cream text, brass for what you touch, an
LED-green for what is connected or verified.

**Derivation.** The warm cast of today's palette pushed further (`ground #161311`, text `#F5EFE3`);
`accent #E8B84D` is brass — yellower and calmer than the current amber, 10:1 on ground; `live
#66D68A` is the green of a meter's "OK" LED, 10.2:1; wash/ink/border are the same green at
§2's wash steps; `error #F3705A` is a warm coral so it never reads as brass.

**App icon.** Works as-is compositionally (brass jaws, green bar) and is the warmest of the three;
regenerate for the new hexes.

**Honest downside.** It is the least change: brass is ~8° from the current amber, so at a glance the
app looks "the same, slightly yellower", and brass + green together carry a retro-instrument
(multimeter, 1970s oscilloscope) flavour that can fight the Liquid-Glass / HIG direction in §11.

## 4. Palette 3 — Concrete (original), cool · accent `#CBEF3F` · live `#BBA4FF`

**Concept.** A concrete shop floor: cool neutral greys a step lighter than today, hi-vis lime (road
paint, safety vest) for what you tap, a violet — the lime's complement — for what is verified.

**Derivation.** `ground #1B1D20` and surfaces are neutral-cool and 2–3 points lighter than the
current ones (concrete, not coal); `accent #CBEF3F` is the brightest tappable here at 12.8:1 and
the loudest; `live #BBA4FF` was lifted one step during the check so its pill text clears 4.5:1 on a
light-photo scrim (was `#B49BFF` at 4.34); `error #FF6E64` is clearly not lime or violet.

**App icon.** Lime jaws are the most visible of the three at 29 px; the violet reading bar is the
thinnest element and greys out at small sizes — regenerate and check the 180 px apple-touch icon.

**Honest downside.** Violet as "verified" has no shop precedent (nothing on a bench is violet), it is
the dimmest live colour once dimmed to 60 % (1.60 / 3.57:1 vs halo), and lime makes the Export
capsule the loudest object on every screen.

## 7. Overlay check (accent / live over the halo on a light and a dark photo)

halo does the work on the light photo in every palette (bare-photo ratios of 1.1–1.7 are why §5

current tokens score the same — and the dimmed state is meant to recede; the selected (full
opacity) row is the legibility state. Concrete's violet is the dimmest live once dimmed;
Night Bench's green and today's teal are the strongest.

## 8. Recommendation

**Dark Sky.** The app lives beside Fusion on the same bench and its two meanings map exactly onto
what the owner already reads there all day — orange is the thing you tap, blue is sketch geometry
and selection — so the palette needs no learning. It is the only one of the three that changes the
app's temperature (cool instead of warm) without touching a single luminance step, so every §2
contrast note and the §5 halo scheme carry over with margins within a point of today's. Night
Bench is the safe runner-up if the warm cast must stay, but it is close enough to the current
amber that the change would go unnoticed, and Concrete's violet asks the user to accept a meaning
no bench colour has. The one condition on Dark Sky is the icon: regenerate it with `#FF7F2A`, not
the raw `#FF6B00`, so it is kin to Fusion rather than a copy. Apply it as the diff below, then
update the same hexes in `src/app/annotate/Draw.res` (`amber`, `amberInk`, `teal`, `handleFill`,
`halo`, `scrim`, `ground`), `src/app/export/Render.res` (`haloColor`, `lineColor`, `pillFillColor`,
`pillTextColor`, `pillBorderColor`), `scripts/make-icons.mjs` (`GROUND`, `AMBER`, `TEAL`), any
`theme-color` in `index.html` and `theme_color`/`background_color` in `public/manifest.json` (all `#17181A` → `#15181D`), and the face-card scrim gradient hardcoded as `rgba(26, 27, 29, …)`
in `part-a.html` when layout A lands.
